Commentary

03/11/2026

Both crude curves remain in steep backwardation. Brent's front spread (January minus February 2027) was +$3.07 on 2 November, and the full twelve-month slope (January 2027 minus December 2027) was +$19.79. WTI is less steep: +$1.45 for December 2026 minus January 2027 and +$16.13 across twelve months on 3 November (intraday prices).

The front of the Brent curve eased over the last two trading days. Comparing the same two contracts, January minus February 2027 narrowed from +$3.87 on 30 October (113.43 − 109.56) to +$3.07 on 2 November (104.77 − 101.70). The fixed-contract chart tells the same story: the Brent and WTI six-month spreads both peaked in late October and have come off since, Brent from around $15 to +$11.71.

Physical crude is still at a premium. On 27 October, Dated Brent was $9.22 above the nearest Brent future (123.10 − 113.88), and $19.73 above WTI spot (123.10 − 103.37), several times its usual gap. WTI's M1–M2 spread sits at the 96th percentile of its 2010–2024 history.

Notes on method

  • Brent and WTI curves can come from different days: each uses its latest day with all 12 contracts priced.
  • If the tracker ran during US trading hours, the latest futures prices are live rather than closing prices.
  • EIA spot prices run a few days behind; the WTI percentile deliberately compares today with 2010-2024.
